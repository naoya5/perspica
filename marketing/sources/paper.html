A Field Study of Local-First Writing Tools

Harbor Notes Working Paper No. 7

Abstract. We study how writers use tools that keep their text on their own computer. Twelve participants used a local translation tool for four weeks. They translated more private documents than before, and they reported less hesitation before pasting sensitive text. We describe the study, the measurements and the limits of the results.

1. Introduction

Translation tools are part of daily work for many people who read and write in two languages. Most of these tools send the text to a remote service. This is a problem for documents that must stay private, such as contracts, medical notes and unpublished research.

Recent small language models can run on a personal computer. This makes it possible to translate text without a network connection. In this paper, we ask a simple question: does a local tool change what people are willing to translate?

2. Method

We recruited twelve participants who translate between Japanese and English at least once a day. Each participant used the local tool for four weeks. The tool recorded only the number of translations and their length. It did not record the text.

3. Results

The number of translations per day increased during the study. Participants said that they stopped asking themselves whether a document was safe to paste. Several participants also said that the tool was useful during travel, when the network was slow or not available.

4. Limits

The group was small and all participants were experienced computer users. A larger study is necessary before these results can be applied to other groups.
