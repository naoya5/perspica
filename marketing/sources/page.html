Harbor NotesAn essay on local-first tools

Software that stays
on your desk.

The usual path

Most of the tools we use every day send our words to a computer that belongs to someone else. A private letter or a draft contract travels across the network before we see the result.

A different path

A local-first tool does the work on the machine in front of you. Nothing is uploaded, so the tool keeps working on a train, on a plane or in a room with no signal.

Why it matters now

Small language models now run on a laptop. When the model is this close, privacy stops being a promise in a policy document. It becomes a property of the design.
